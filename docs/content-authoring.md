# Content authoring checklist

Use this when replacing placeholders with real portfolio information. The Angular app does not currently load these values; this guide defines the content to prepare for the first implementation.

## Profile

- Name and preferred professional title
- One specific sentence about who you help and what outcome you create
- Short summary written for a hiring manager or potential client
- City/time zone, if useful
- Professional email and relevant public links
- Portrait image with permission to publish it and useful alt text
- Résumé URL or downloadable file

## Experience

For each role, record title, organization, location, dates, scope, and two or three contributions. Lead with outcomes and explain your own role. Use numbers only when you can substantiate them. Clearly label contract, freelance, volunteer, and personal work.

## Skills

Group skills into a few meaningful categories (for example craft, strategy, tools, and collaboration). Prioritize skills relevant to the roles or projects you want. Let projects and experience demonstrate proficiency rather than using arbitrary progress bars.

## Project case studies

For each project, prepare:

1. Context: organization, audience, timeframe, and constraints.
2. Challenge: what needed to change and why it mattered.
3. Contribution: what you personally owned and how you worked with others.
4. Decisions: the most important trade-off or insight.
5. Outcome: measurable evidence or a clear qualitative result.
6. Media: cover image, alt text, image rights, and any redactions required.

Get permission before publishing client work, internal metrics, screenshots, testimonials, or identifiable customer information. If permission is not available, anonymize only when doing so still represents the work honestly; otherwise omit the material.

## About story

Use a few specific moments rather than a full life history:

- Where your interest or career began
- A turning point that influenced how you work
- What you care about learning or doing next
- One or two genuine interests outside work

Keep personal details voluntary and appropriate for a public professional site.

## Blog posts (later)

Each post should have a unique title and slug, concise excerpt, author, publish date, optional update date, tags, cover media and alt text, and body content. Add a clear editorial status such as draft, scheduled, or published. Public pages should expose only published posts.
